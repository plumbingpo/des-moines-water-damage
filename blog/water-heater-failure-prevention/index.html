Water Heater Maintenance Guide in Des Moines, IA

Home / Blog / Water Heater Failure Prevention in Des Moines, IA

Water heater failures are one of the most common causes of residential water damage in Des Moines, IA. When a typical 40 to 50 gallon tank lets go all at once, every gallon can be on your floor within minutes, and the harm to walls, flooring, and stored items can run into the thousands of dollars. If you know the warning signs and keep up with prevention, you can avoid this costly emergency.

Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Fail

Watch for these indicators that your water heater may be nearing failure: age over 8 to 12 years (the typical lifespan for tank-style heaters), rust-colored water from hot taps, rumbling or popping sounds during heating cycles, visible corrosion or rust on the tank exterior, puddles or moisture around the base of the unit, reduced hot water capacity, and a relief valve that drips or discharges frequently.

Maintenance That Prevents Failure

Regular maintenance extends water heater life and reduces failure risk: flush the tank annually to remove sediment buildup. The anode rod needs a look every 2 to 3 years and should be swapped out once it is significantly corroded. The pressure and temperature relief valve should be tested every year. Worn or corroded supply connections are worth checking for as well. Leave open space around the unit so it can be inspected easily. A leak detection sensor placed by the base is worth considering.

Is It Time for a New Water Heater?

The most effective way to prevent a catastrophic water heater failure is proactive replacement before the unit fails. If your water heater is over 10 years old, showing signs of corrosion, or requiring frequent repairs, replacing it now prevents a future flood. You will spend much less on a scheduled swap than on emergency water damage restoration. With a tankless model, there is no tank to fail, so think about that upgrade.

Steps to Take After a Water Heater Flood

If your water heater fails, shut off the water supply to the unit immediately (the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater), then shut off the main water supply as backup.
